It's a statutory duty, not a formality

Under Regulation 22 of the Electricity Safety, Quality and Continuity Regulations 2002, no one may operate generation in parallel with a public distribution network without the DNO's agreement. Below 16 A per phase there's an exemption — you notify. Above it, you must have agreement first.

Non-compliance is an offence

Regulation 35 makes failure to comply with Regulation 22 an offence carrying a fine — unlimited in England and Wales, capped at £5,000 in Scotland. It applies to any person, which means the installer as well as the generator.

The DNO can disconnect you

G99 is explicit that connection is only allowed once the application is approved. Electricity North West put it plainly in their own guidance: if they don't believe your generation is compliant, "you will be disconnected from the network until full compliance can be determined."

No record, no export payments

The DNO creates your export MPAN off the back of the G98/G99 record. No approval means no export MPAN, and without an export MPAN no supplier can pay you under the Smart Export Guarantee — however good your MCS certificate is.

What £250 actually buys

The failure modes we take off your desk.

Applications rarely fail because the system is wrong. They fail because of the paperwork around it. These are the specific things that send a form back — every one of them named by DNOs themselves.

  1. 01

    Validate eligibility

    We establish whether you're in G98, SGI-1, SGI-2, SGI-3 or above — and check the traps: intrinsic design capacity per unit, whether a G100 limiter is required at 16 A or 32 A, and whether island/EPS capability takes you off the fast track altogether.

  2. 02

    Prepare the application

    We complete Form A1-2 with the manufacturer's reference number from the ENA register — the single most common omission DNOs complain about — and we check the type-test entry is the current version, not a superseded one. We produce the single-line diagram and the written G100 fail-safe description that must go with it.

  3. 03

    Submit to the right operator

    Fourteen licence areas, six groups, and a growing list of independent networks — each with its own route, portal and inbox. We read the distributor ID from the MPAN so a new-build on an IDNO network doesn't sit in the wrong queue for a month.

  4. 04

    Chase it to a decision

    We deal with the DNO directly, answer their queries, and keep you posted. If they escalate off the fast track we tell you straight away and explain what changed. When you commission, we file the A3-2 notification inside the 28-day window so the record is closed properly.

What we can and can't promise

We can't promise a network operator will say yes, or that they won't take your job off the fast track and onto a full assessment — those are their decisions, not ours, and anyone telling you otherwise is selling you something. What we promise is that the application goes in right, and that we stay with it until there's a decision.

If you're an installer, the application is lodged in your name and under your accreditation — we're doing your paperwork, not taking your job. If you're a property owner, we can handle the whole thing: the application first, because you legally need it, and the installation after.
