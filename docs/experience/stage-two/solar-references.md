# Solar reference study — 8 September 2026

The rendered Sun is an original cinematic scientific illustration. It combines structural cues seen by instruments at different wavelengths, with compressed scale and timing. Its gold palette is authored; it does not reproduce one instrument's wavelength, natural colour, or a literal human-eye view. None of the reference pixels are served by the experience.

## 1. Photosphere: irregular cells and bright magnetic points

- Source: [NSO, cropped first-light image from DKIST](https://nso.edu/telescopes/dkist/first-light-cropped-image/), released with the January 2020 first-light material.
- Exact image credit: **NSO/NSF/AURA**.
- Instrument/view: 789 nm; the cropped field covers 8,200 × 8,200 km. The page explains bright rising cell interiors, dark descending lanes, and tiny bright magnetic features in the lanes. This is a contrast-processed scientific image, not an unprocessed visible-light colour photograph.
- Inspected image: [source low-resolution JPEG](https://nso1.b-cdn.net/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Crop-Image-low.jpg), downloaded 2026-09-08 to `/private/tmp/aesir-solar-granulation.jpg` for private art-direction review; untransformed, 62,121 bytes. No source asset added to the build.
- Image-specific terms: the page lists CC BY 4.0 while also retaining Science Verification Phase restrictions for scientific/publication/outreach use. The [NSO policy](https://nso.edu/about/image-use-policy/) requires the full visible credit and disallows implied endorsement. We use structural observations only, not reproduction.
- Art direction from inspection: uneven creamy cells separated by narrow dark creases, with much smaller bright intersections. Preserve dark separations instead of making every cell orange-white. At globe scale the cells must be fine; large active areas are a separate lower-frequency layer.

## 2. Prominence: one attached, asymmetric focal structure

- Source: [NASA SVS item 4277, limb prominence on 12 July 2011](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4277/), visualization published 2015-03-03; observations 2011-07-12, 02:00–06:00.
- Exact requested credit: **NASA's Scientific Visualization Studio, the SDO Science Team, and the Virtual Solar Observatory.** Visualization: Tom Bridgman.
- Instrument/view: SDO/AIA 304 Å extreme ultraviolet. The recorded plasma rises, pauses and returns along magnetic structure. This is not a visible-light photosphere reference.
- Inspected image: [1024 × 576 reference still](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a000000/a004200/a004277/July2011Prominence_304A_stand.HD1080i.00600_print.jpg), downloaded 2026-09-08 to `/private/tmp/aesir-solar-prominence.jpg`; untransformed, 94,578 bytes. No source pixels served.
- Terms: item links to [NASA media guidance](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/). Source attribution and no implied endorsement; third-party restrictions must be checked image by image. This item shows no separate third-party copyright restriction.
- Art direction from inspection: the upper-left structure has a broad luminous attachment, a faint lofted canopy and uneven strands. Most of the limb is comparatively quiet. Use one dominant prominence, one smaller neighbour and a quieter opposite limb; roots should merge into active surface areas. Avoid uniformly spaced identical wires.

## 3. Corona: directional, soft structure at several scales

- Source: [ESA, The Sun’s fluffy corona in exquisite detail](https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Videos/2024/04/The_Sun_s_fluffy_corona_in_exquisite_detail), published 2024-05-02; Solar Orbiter/EUI observation 2023-09-27.
- Exact credit: **ESA & NASA/Solar Orbiter/EUI Team**.
- Terms on this specific item: **CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO** or ESA Standard Licence. This is a reference only; no footage is redistributed or modified.
- Instrument caveat: extreme-ultraviolet atmospheric imagery. Brightness traces hot plasma; cooler absorbing structures can be dark. Do not present the gold colouring as natural colour, or all dark structures as photospheric sunspots.
- Inspected the [unannotated page's preview](https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Videos/2024/04/The_Sun_s_fluffy_corona_in_exquisite_detail_-_unannotated_version), downloaded 2026-09-08 to `/private/tmp/aesir-solar-corona.jpg`; no build copy. The film itself was not downloaded or motion-reviewed.
- Art direction from the still: broad low-contrast swaths share a direction; finer curved strands gather around rooted bright patches; an isolated central eruption becomes the focal point because the surrounding atmosphere stays soft. Separate slow corona drift, medium prominence deformation and fine surface shimmer. Keep all three moving on elapsed time while scroll only changes the camera.

## Practical comparison when reviewing the render

The close-up should keep readable dark structure in the surface, sparse cream-white hot points and a prominence visibly attached at both roots. The corona should extend those field directions softly into darkness. Increasing bloom globally would erase exactly these distinctions; adjust spatial scale, anisotropy and colour/exposure first.
